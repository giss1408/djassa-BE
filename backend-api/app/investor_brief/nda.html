Between the undersigned

Fidelia SAS, a simplified joint-stock company with share capital of amount FCFA, registered with the Abidjan Trade and Personal Property Credit Register under number RCCM, with registered office at address, Abidjan, Côte d’Ivoire, represented by Mr Stanislas Regisse, President, hereinafter “Fidelia”,

and name, legal form, capital, registration number, registered office, represented by name, title, duly authorised (or, for an individual: full name, date of birth, address), hereinafter “the Partner”,

together “the Parties” and each “a Party”.

Recitals

The Parties intend to exchange information for the following purpose: e.g. assessing a payment partnership, evaluating a pilot with a microfinance institution, evaluating an investment, a development assignment (the “Project”). These exchanges involve confidential information, which the Parties agree to protect on the terms below.

Article 1: Confidential information

1.1. “Confidential Information” means any information of any kind disclosed by one Party (the “Disclosing Party”) to the other (the “Receiving Party”) in connection with the Project, in any form (written, oral, electronic, demonstration, access to software or to a code repository), including:

  • the concept, business model, strategy, roadmaps, pricing and financial assumptions;
  • source code, technical architecture, programming interfaces, databases and documentation;
  • data about merchants, customers, partners and transactions;
  • financial, legal and commercial information, including the existence and content of the Parties’ discussions.

1.2. Information disclosed orally is confidential if its confidential nature is stated when it is disclosed, or reasonably follows from its nature or the circumstances.

Article 2: Exclusions

Information is not confidential if the Receiving Party can show that it:

  • a) was public when disclosed, or later became public through no fault of its own;
  • b) was already lawfully in its possession without a duty of confidentiality;
  • c) was lawfully received from a third party not bound by confidentiality;
  • d) was independently developed without use of the Confidential Information;
  • e) must be disclosed by law, regulation (including the BCEAO’s) or a judicial or administrative order; the Receiving Party then notifies the Disclosing Party in advance where the law allows, and limits disclosure to what is strictly required.

Article 3: Obligations of the Receiving Party

The Receiving Party undertakes to:

  • 3.1. use the Confidential Information solely for the Project;
  • 3.2. not disclose it to any third party without the Disclosing Party’s prior written consent;
  • 3.3. share it only with those of its officers, employees, advisers and subcontractors who need it for the Project, are told it is confidential and are bound by an obligation at least as protective, and to be responsible for their compliance;
  • 3.4. protect it with the same care as its own confidential information, and at least with reasonable care;
  • 3.5. not copy, reproduce, decompile or disassemble it, in particular software, except as strictly necessary for the Project;
  • 3.6. not use it to design, develop or market, directly or indirectly, a product or service competing with the Disclosing Party’s;
  • 3.7. promptly notify the Disclosing Party of any unauthorised disclosure or use it becomes aware of.

Article 4: Personal data

If personal data (for example customers’ phone numbers) is exchanged, the Parties process it in accordance with Ivorian law, in particular Law No. 2013-450 of 19 June 2013 on the protection of personal data, and ARTCI requirements. No real personal data is exchanged before a separate data-processing agreement is signed where one is required.

Article 5: Ownership, no licence

5.1. Confidential Information remains the exclusive property of the Disclosing Party. 5.2. This agreement grants no ownership, licence or other right in the Confidential Information or in any trademark, patent, copyright or other intellectual property of the Disclosing Party. 5.3. Confidential Information is provided “as is”, without warranty of accuracy or completeness.

Article 6: No obligation to proceed

This agreement does not oblige either Party to enter into any further agreement, to pursue the Project or to disclose any particular information. Either Party may end the discussions at any time.

Article 7: Return and destruction

At the end of the Project, or on the Disclosing Party’s written request, the Receiving Party returns or destroys all Confidential Information and copies within fifteen (15) days and confirms this in writing. Copies held in automatic backups, or retained as required by law, remain subject to this agreement.

Article 8: Non-solicitation (optional clause)

During the Project and for twelve (12) months after it ends, neither Party solicits for employment an employee or contractor of the other Party met through the Project, without the other Party’s written consent.

Article 9: Term

9.1. This agreement takes effect on the date of the last signature and covers information disclosed in connection with the Project before or after that date. 9.2. Confidentiality obligations last three (3) years from the end of the Project or termination of this agreement; for source code and trade secrets, for as long as the information remains confidential.

Article 10: Remedies

A breach may cause the Disclosing Party harm that damages alone cannot adequately repair. In addition to damages, the Disclosing Party may seek interim relief (référé) to stop the breach.

Article 11: General provisions

This agreement is the entire agreement of the Parties on its subject and may be amended only in writing signed by both. It may not be assigned without the other Party’s written consent. If a clause is void, the others remain in force. Failure to enforce a breach is not a waiver. It may be signed electronically and in counterparts.

Article 12: Governing law and disputes

12.1. This agreement is governed by Ivorian law, including the applicable OHADA Uniform Acts. 12.2. The Parties first seek an amicable settlement for thirty (30) days; failing that, the dispute is submitted to the Abidjan Commercial Court.

Option to confirm with a lawyer: arbitration under the Rules of the OHADA Common Court of Justice and Arbitration (CCJA), seat Abidjan, language French.
