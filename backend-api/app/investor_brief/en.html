Fidelia turns everyday sales at neighbourhood shops into proof: a verified business history that the merchant owns and, with consent, can take to a licensed lender. Merchants pay for it because it brings their customers back.

Problem

  • 25M+ active mobile-money accounts, but a 31.2% strict banking rate (BCEAO; national indicators 2023). Payment access is solved; proof of activity is not.
  • A maquis or grocery shop has frequent customers but no usable sales history, so no stock or working-capital credit. SMEs are ~20% of GDP.
  • Loyalty lives on paper cards and memory. Nothing brings a regular back on purpose.

Product

  • One daily cash book from day 1: every wallet payment and every cash sale in one daily total, with no reconciling Wave, Orange and MTN by hand.
  • New customers for the merchant: a customer app with a map of nearby maquis and grocery shops, free offers with notifications, points, and on-duty pharmacies as the daily reason to install it.
  • Zero extra effort on mobile money: payments to the QR the merchant already uses are captured automatically. Wave first, then other operators and the interoperable QR of PI-SPI, the central bank’s platform linking every bank and mobile wallet in the UEMOA zone. No extra fee.
  • Cash counts too: one tap in the merchant app, even offline. With the customer’s phone number, the customer earns points.
  • Loyalty the merchant sees: points, rewards at the counter, deals, and a weekly “customers who came back” figure.
  • Proof for credit, with consent: each sale is labelled confirmed or declared, and exported only with consent to a licensed MFI or guarantee scheme.

Fidelia never holds funds and never lends. Licensed institutions keep custody and credit.

Why now

  • 2.72M merchant payment points in Côte d’Ivoire (2024, from 1.13M); merchant payments are 23.3% of mobile-money volume (from 3.3% in 2020).
  • Payment acceptance is a commodity (~1% on Wave). Recognising customers and proving activity are not.
  • The BCEAO has opened alternative-scoring work and PI-SPI; public programmes (APIF, SGPME) seek private execution partners.
  • Comparables: merchant credit from payment data was followed by +36% (Moniepoint) and +42% (Kopo Kopo) transaction growth.

Business model

  • Merchant subscription after a free pilot: capped free plan, then ~5,000 F and ~10,000–15,000 F / month / outlet (hypotheses)
  • After the pilot: featured deals in the customer app, and campaigns
  • Later: network contracts; consented partner commissions (credit, savings, tontine), with no credit risk carried

Illustrative: +5% from returning customers ≈ 73,000 F gross profit a month for a small maquis, against a 5,000 F subscription.

Traction

Pre-pilot: no users, merchants or revenue yet. Built as prototypes:

  • Offline merchant app, tested on a real device
  • Customer app: discovery, on-duty pharmacies, deals, points
  • Cash-sale points by phone; rewards at the counter
  • One sale history labelled by evidence; billing ledger
