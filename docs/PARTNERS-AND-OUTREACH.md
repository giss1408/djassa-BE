# Djassa Partners and Outreach Plan

This document describes who to contact, why to contact them, and what evidence to bring. Verify current names, mandates, and contact details on official websites before formal outreach.

## Partner categories

| Category | Why it matters | First conversation |
|---|---|---|
| Regulatory and public institutions | Confirm the legal perimeter and public-program alignment | Explain the pilot, data flows, and planned financial features |
| Merchant networks | Recruit pilot outlets and validate workflow | Demonstrate onboarding, loyalty, and reporting |
| Microfinance institutions and banks | Hold funds, provide savings or credit, and validate use cases | Present consented merchant history and referral model |
| Payment aggregators | Connect mobile-money rails through an approved integration | Confirm APIs, settlement, webhook, fees, and support |
| Mobile-money operators | Provide commercial and technical payment capabilities | Confirm merchant products, limits, and partnership requirements |
| Guarantee programs and development partners | Reduce financing risk and support inclusion pilots | Present target users, measurement plan, and safeguards |
| Fintech ecosystem groups | Improve visibility and regulatory awareness | Join working groups and obtain introductions |

## Recommended first contacts

### 1. APIF-CI

**Purpose:** Validate alignment with the national financial-inclusion strategy and identify public or institutional pilot channels.

**Ask for:** A discovery meeting, relevant programs, and introductions to implementation partners.

### 2. APSFD-CI

**Purpose:** Reach multiple microfinance institutions through one sector entry point.

**Ask for:** Guidance on the right partner profile and introductions to institutions serving merchants, women, and rural communities.

### 3. CIFA

**Purpose:** Understand the Ivorian fintech ecosystem and regulatory direction.

**Ask for:** Ecosystem events, working groups, and introductions to payment and finance partners.

### 4. CinetPay or another approved payment aggregator

**Purpose:** Validate the first payment integration and operational model.

**Ask for:** Sandbox access, webhook specifications, settlement rules, transaction limits, pricing, reconciliation, and incident support.

### 5. One focused microfinance partner

Potential profiles include a network serving microbusinesses, women entrepreneurs, or rural businesses.

**Purpose:** Test whether structured merchant history is useful in a real financing workflow.

**Ask for:** A small, consent-based pilot with clearly defined eligibility, data fields, decisions, and feedback.

### 6. Relevant regulator or legal adviser

Engage the BCEAO or qualified local regulatory counsel before any feature that moves money, holds funds, produces a score for third parties, or offers credit.

**Purpose:** Confirm whether Djassa acts as a technology provider, agent, payment facilitator, or another regulated role.

## Outreach sequence

1. Prepare a two-page concept note and a working product demo.
2. Interview five to ten merchants before approaching large institutions.
3. Contact APIF-CI, APSFD-CI, and CIFA for ecosystem validation.
4. Request payment sandbox access and technical documentation.
5. Select one financial partner for a narrow pilot.
6. Define consent, data-sharing, reconciliation, support, and incident processes.
7. Run the pilot and publish measured results to prospective partners.

## Channel strategy

The lowest-cost acquisition path is likely a trusted channel rather than direct consumer advertising. Test these channels in order:

1. Merchant associations and cooperatives.
2. Payment aggregators and mobile-money merchant networks.
3. Distributors serving pharmacies, groceries, salons, and restaurants.
4. Microfinance institutions that already serve the target merchants.
5. Telecom or messaging partnerships after the workflow is proven.

For each channel, measure merchants activated, merchants still active after 30/90 days, support cost, and revenue per acquired outlet. A partnership is valuable only if it lowers acquisition or support cost while preserving user trust.

## What to bring to every meeting

- The problem in one paragraph.
- The target user and geographic scope.
- The current product and what is actually implemented.
- A simple data-flow diagram.
- The proposed role of each partner.
- Revenue model and fee transparency.
- Privacy, consent, security, and incident controls.
- Pilot duration, success metrics, and exit criteria.
- Specific decision or introduction requested from the partner.

## Questions for partners

### Regulatory

- Which activities require authorization or must be performed by a licensed institution?
- Can the proposed pilot operate in a sandbox or controlled test framework?
- What consent, retention, audit, and data-transfer requirements apply?

### Technical

- Is there a sandbox environment?
- How are signatures, retries, idempotency, reconciliation, and settlement handled?
- What uptime, support, and incident-notification commitments exist?
- Which identifiers are stable across retries and channels?

### Commercial

- Who pays the fee and when?
- What is the minimum volume or contract term?
- What happens when a customer withdraws consent?
- Which outcomes define a successful pilot?

## Outreach guardrails

- Do not claim that Djassa provides loans.
- Do not promise approval, better interest rates, or guaranteed savings returns.
- Do not send real customer data in a first meeting.
- Use synthetic or anonymized pilot data until a written data-sharing agreement exists.
- Verify every public statistic and institutional contact before publishing it.
- Do not describe a continent-wide rollout. Present a specific country, city/corridor, merchant segment, and pilot volume.
